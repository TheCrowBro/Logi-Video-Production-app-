# Where the price list's typical rates come from

Vdex's price list (Settings → Quotes & invoices → 🏷 Edit the price list) starts from typical freelance rates for the region your currency belongs to. This page lists every figure with the note and the sources behind it.

**These are starting points, not advice.** They describe what working freelancers charge small-business, corporate, event and wedding clients: neither the cheapest nor premium rates, and not union scale for feature films. Most figures come from 2025–2026 sources; a few older figures were adjusted for inflation, and the notes say so. Your market, city, experience, kit and the job decide your price. Every price in the list is yours to change.

How they were made, in November 2026:

- For each region, a researcher gathered published rates: crew rate cards and surveys (in the UK, Bectu and the APA), freelance rate guides and cost guides, job postings with day rates, hire houses' published kit prices, stock music and footage libraries' price pages, and the tax office's mileage rate.
- A second check then re-opened the cited pages and nearby pages on the same sites, corrected figures and notes that the pages did not support, and tested that the figures hang together (a half day against a day, an editing day against eight hours, hire against crew rates). It did not run fresh web searches, though it found a few new sources on those sites, and some thin items could not be re-checked; each region's section says what was and was not checked.
- A final review compared the regions with each other and spot-checked surprising figures against fresh sources; the changes it led to are listed under each region.
- Where a source used another unit (an hour instead of a day, or another currency), the figure was converted and the note says so.
- Many sources are vendor pages, marketplace listings and small or anonymous websites rather than independent surveys. Treat the figures as indications.

Confidence: **good** means several consistent sources; **fair** means some sources or a converted figure; **thin** means few published rates, so the figure is an informed estimate. In the app, the editor says so for thin lines.

Hire lines (camera, lights, audio, gimbal) are what you would bill a client for a day's use of the kit. In most places that tracks the rental houses' day rates; your own kit may be cheaper or dearer.

Prices are per the unit shown, before any tax (VAT, GST, sales tax).

### Notes and sources

- **Filming day: videographer with camera kit (up to 10 hours).** Videographer with their own camera kit for a 10-hour day. 2026 guides put mid-level freelancers at $600-$1,200, $800-$1,500 or $800-$2,000, and beginners at $250-$800. The typical figure sits mid-market. Large metros run higher. Sources: <https://vidico.com/news/how-much-do-videographers-charge/> · <https://ruahcreativehouse.org/blog/freelance-videographer-rates/> · <https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/halfday-rates-around-the-us/>

### What the checks changed, and caveats

- Changes made in the check: sound typical $950 to $1,000 and high $1,400 to $1,500 (the 2026 rate card is $900 labour plus a $500 interview package, not $200 for kit). Social cut typical $175 to $150 (guides price a basic short at $40-$150, and a reformat is less work). Raw handover changed from $100/$250/$600 to $150/$350/$1,000 (a wedding guide lists $500-$1,500). Rush high $750 to $1,000 (the cited guide now says 25-100%, not 20-50%).
- Notes corrected without changing the numbers: Shutterstock is $69 HD and $99 4K, about $80 each in a 4K 5-pack, not '$79 or more, $120 each in a 5-pack'. Pond5 is $49 standard and $149 premium. 3Play captions cost $0.90-$5.00 a minute and translations $4-$19. Musicbed publishes only 'from $49 per project', not '$49-$199 single songs'. The archive sources give no per-TB or AU$50-200 figure. The audio hire rate card lists a $500 interview package, not $200. The Lensrentals FX6 kit is about $121 a day. Crew-rate table figures are for a 12-hour day.
- Sources removed because they no longer load or give no figures: the Sheridan College tech-scout page (404), the fangdagger price page (404), the jrbyers pricing page ('available on request') and the C&I Studios article (no prices). The '$150 tech scout fee' and '$500 flat 24-hour rush' claims could not be confirmed and were taken out of the notes.
- How it was checked: the cited pages were re-opened and checked directly; no new sources were searched for. Checked against the live pages: shoot day, half day, hourly, sound, gaffer, director, DIT, edit day, camera hire, gimbal hire, audio hire, prompter, grade, motion, captions, stock clips, music, social cut, raw handover, wedding, mileage, travel time, pre-production, recce, revision, rush and archive.
- Some pages blocked automated reading or did not respond (The Knot, Backstage, fash.com, AIR, PremiumBeat, KitSplit, the StaffMeUp drone post, Candid Studios, Thumbtack, dayrates-org). Figures from those sources were left as they were and were not re-verified. The Knot figures were confirmed through a secondary source.
- Consistency checks passed: the half day is 62.5% of the day rate, edit day equals 8 times the hourly rate, travel time is half of the day rate divided by 10, and the recce costs less than a half shoot day. Kit-only hire items all sit below crew day rates. The teleprompter ($950) and sound ($1,000) cost more than some labour-only crew because they include an operator and gear.
- Most sources are vendor blogs, marketplace listings and job posts, not audited surveys. Treat the ranges as starting points to adjust locally.
- Market matters a lot. NYC, LA and SF run about 30-60% above these figures, and small markets run below.
- Crew day rates assume a 10-hour day unless the note says 12 hours. Overtime is usually 1.5 times the rate. Union-scale rates are excluded.
- Hire items (camera, lights, audio, gimbal) are what a freelancer bills a client as a kit fee. Rental-house costs to the freelancer are lower, especially on weekly rentals.
- Mileage changed during 2026: the IRS rate was 72.5 cents per mile until 30 June and has been 76 cents since 1 July. Check for the 2027 rate, usually announced in December.
- Music and stock footage prices are vendor list prices and change often. Client or brand work may need a business or enhanced licence that costs more.
- The Storyhunter director and producer figures were seen only in a search summary; the page itself was blocked.
- Some figures are estimates, not published rates: the client markup on captions, flat prices for a revision round, flat rush fees, archiving and the half-day recce price.
- The Knot ($2,300 average) and Zola ($3,993 average) wedding figures disagree. The typical wedding price sits between them.
- After a further review (November 2026): the music note now uses PremiumBeat's current per-track prices ($39, $59 and $199) and drops a page that no longer exists.

- **Filming day: videographer with camera kit (up to 10 hours).** Videographer with standard camera, light and audio kit, about 10 hours. On Point Video (Toronto) still lists $900 including kit, on a page last updated in February 2024. Viva Media's 2026 guide gives a DP $800–1,500. Codex (Vancouver) starts at $800. Codex (Vancouver) starts at $575 for a half day against $800 for a full day. A forum example puts a $1,000/day shooter at about $650 for a half day, with big-city half days at $700–900. Sources: <https://codex-studios.pages.dev/pricing> · <https://creativecow.net/forums/thread/halfday-rates-around-the-us/>

### What the checks changed, and caveats

- Changes made in the check: sound typical went from $950 to $1,000 and high from $1,150 to $1,200 (the rate-card median is $1,000, and Sydney goes up to $1,275). Mix typical went from $110 to $100 (two published AU rate cards both say $100/hr). Recce high went from $750 to $800 to match Jasper's $300–$800 for location scouting, and recce confidence went from thin to fair. DIT high went from $750 to $850 (a dedicated DIT costs $510–$825 labour only). Raw handover typical went from $250 to $300 and high from $600 to $750 (US$200–$500 is about A$305–$765). Motion and social cut-down confidence went from thin to fair.
- Corrected notes: editing per hour wrongly gave Solohourly's mid-level rate as $70–$90. That figure is Curated Visuals'; Solohourly's mid-level is A$38–$65. event filming cited a $690–$2,300 half-day figure that is not on the cited page; the page gives $2,500–$6,000 for a full event day. Prompter described the US$400–$700 figure as operator labour, but it already includes the teleprompter kit. Wedding cited Duuet package prices that could not be verified, so they were removed. iSeekPlant drone rates are from an April 2022 article. The iSeekPlant URL now uses its redirect target.
- Every other item was re-checked against its cited source and kept unchanged. The internal checks pass: the half day is 65% of the full day, the edit day ($750) is close to 8 × the hourly rate ($95 × 8 = $760), every hire price is below the matching crew rate, and low ≤ typical ≤ high holds for every item.
- How it was checked: the cited source pages (more than 25) were re-fetched directly; no new sources were searched for. The ATO cents-per-km page could not be opened.
- All figures are in AUD and exclude GST (10% in Australia, 15% in New Zealand).
- No New Zealand rate data was gathered, apart from the IRD kilometre rate. NZ freelancers usually charge similar or slightly lower numbers in NZD, which is roughly 10–15% less in AUD.
- Many sources are vendor blogs and pricing guides (Vidico, Jasper Pictures, Queen Charles, Curated Visuals, Beverly Boy), not independent surveys. Beverly Boy does not say whether its crew rates are AUD or USD. Sound recordist and rental-house prices come from published rate cards and are the most reliable.
- Rental-house prices from Videocraft exclude GST and insurance. Freelancers billing their own gear often charge less than rental houses.
- USD figures were converted at about 1.53 AUD per USD; adjust to the current exchange rate.
- Items still marked thin (gaffer, director, pre-production, teleprompter, grade, captions, rush, travel time, DIT, raw footage hand-over, archive) are estimates derived from related rates, not published AU line-item prices.
