Vdex privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026. Applies to the Vdex web app, the Vdex desktop app for Windows, Mac and Linux and, when they are published, the Vdex apps on Google Play and the App Store.

The short version

  • Vdex has no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and no account for your work. Its only server of its own checks licences (by email sign-in on the website), and never sees your work.
  • Everything you type stays on your phone or computer. The people who make Vdex never receive it.
  • Information leaves your device only when you use a feature that needs it: linking your own cloud storage or calendar, looking up the weather or an address, scanning a page, or sharing something. The cases are listed below. Every service you contact also sees your device's IP address, as any website does.

2. What Vdex stores, and where

Vdex keeps the information you enter in the app's own storage on your device: the browser's storage for the web app, and the app's folder for the desktop app. That information can include:

  • Bookings: the job, the client and their contacts, addresses, dates, the schedule, notes, the hours you worked, the people hired for the job (name, role, rate, days, phone, email, notes) and the items rented in.
  • Call sheets, quotes and invoices made from those bookings.
  • Budgets on your bookings: cost lines with estimates, actual costs and who they are paid to.
  • Stripboards on your bookings: scenes, sets, cast names, page counts and your notes on each strip.
  • Crew: names, roles, phone numbers, email addresses and day or hourly rates, and the list of roles you have typed yourself (My roles).
  • Equipment: items, serial numbers, values, warranties and service history, whether you typed them or imported them from a list.
  • Venues: addresses, access notes and map coordinates.
  • Insurance policies and their dates.
  • Your own profile: your name, contact details, and the QR contact card and card link made from them.
  • Bookings, contacts and equipment you added from a link someone shared with you, each with who the link says shared it and when. A booking added this way also keeps what the link supplied, so that a newer link can update it.
  • Timecode notes, scripts you import, and your settings.

None of this is sent to us. You can see or erase all of it at any time, and save a copy of it once you have a licence, under Settings → Storage, sync & backups. The search in Vdex looks through this information on your device: what you type into it is not sent anywhere and is not saved.

3. Your PIN and fingerprint or face unlock

Vdex asks you to set a PIN (4 to 20 digits) before it can be used, and encrypts its storage with a key derived from that PIN using AES-256-GCM. The PIN itself is never stored, and nobody, including us, can recover it. Vdex keeps only how many digits it has, on this device, so the lock screen can show a dot for each. Fingerprint or face unlock uses your device's own system (WebAuthn). Vdex never sees your fingerprint or your face; it only receives the device's confirmation that it was you.

The PIN protects your work, your sign-ins to cloud storage and calendars, your saved location and your calendar feed's link on this device while Vdex is locked, hidden or closed. It has limits:

  • Someone who copies the device's storage can guess a short PIN. Vdex accepts any PIN of 4 to 20 digits; a longer one is harder to guess.
  • If fingerprint or face unlock keeps its key on the device (Settings → Security says so when it does), someone with a copy of the device's storage may open Vdex's information without the PIN.
  • If the browser's storage is full, Vdex cannot keep the encrypted copy. It says so, and until you free some space your information stays on the device unencrypted.
  • The copy in your cloud storage is kept as plain text and is protected only by your account there. A backup file is plain unless you choose the encrypted kind, and anything you print, export or send is as you sent it.

Some things are kept on the device outside the PIN's protection, so that Vdex can start, show the lock screen and remember your choices: your display and tool settings; how many digits your PIN has; that you agreed to the terms, and when; your licence sign-in or, in the desktop app, your licence code (each holds the email address of your licence; a sign-in from an App Store purchase holds the purchase's number instead), the day your trial began, when a licence code's 14-day wait began and when a licence stopped covering the device (section 4, Licences), and the random number made for the installation; which of the Me page's QR codes you last chose, and which parts you last ticked for a crew link; a link someone shared with you that is waiting to be opened (kept for that browser tab only, until you have unlocked Vdex and seen what it holds); and a file shared to Vdex while it was locked (kept until you next unlock it).

4. When information leaves your device

Each of the following happens only when you switch a feature on or use it. Every service that is contacted also sees your device's IP address, as any website does.

Cloud storage you link

You can link Google Drive, a WebDAV server of your choosing, or a folder on your computer today, and Apple iCloud, Dropbox and Microsoft OneDrive when Vdex offers them. Vdex then saves its database and backup files into your own account, so your other devices can read them and a copy survives a lost phone. That content is held by the provider under its own privacy policy. We have no access to it. With iCloud, the copy is kept in Vdex's container in your private iCloud database through Apple's CloudKit service: it counts toward your iCloud storage, only you can read it, and Apple says it does not access it except as the law requires. In Vdex, “Delete Vdex’s copy from iCloud” removes it; you can also manage iCloud storage in your Apple account settings. Sign-in tokens stay on your device and are encrypted while Vdex is locked. You can unlink the storage in Vdex at any time and withdraw Vdex's access in your provider's account settings. Linking a cloud storage needs a Vdex licence; without one, a storage linked before is paused and Vdex sends it nothing.

Calendar sync and calendar feeds

If you switch on calendar sync, Vdex creates and updates events for your bookings in your own Google Calendar (or Microsoft Outlook calendar, when Vdex offers it): the title, dates, location and a description with the client, the venue, crew names, contacts' names and phone numbers, and your booking notes. The "Add to calendar" buttons either save a calendar file on your device, or open Google Calendar, Outlook or Yahoo Calendar in your browser with the same details written into the address, so that service receives them when its page opens. If you create a calendar feed, Vdex saves a calendar file to your linked storage and asks that provider for a share link (iCloud gives no such link, so there is no feed with iCloud). Anyone who has that link can read the feed, so share it only with the people you want.

Weather

When Vdex shows a day on the calendar, a booking or a call sheet that is within the next 16 days, it asks Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) for that day's forecast, by itself. It sends the coordinates of the booking's venue or, if the venue has none, the location you saved in Vdex, which may be your own, with the date and the units to answer in. The coordinates are rounded to two decimal places first, about a kilometre. With neither a venue on the map nor a saved location, nothing is sent. No names, no account and no identifier are sent. Open-Meteo also sees your internet address, and says it keeps server logs, which can include coordinates, for up to 90 days.

Addresses and maps

When you look up a venue or press "Find on map", Vdex sends the address text to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to turn it into coordinates, and loads map images for the area shown from OpenStreetMap's tile servers. Directions buttons open Google Maps or Apple Maps on your device with the address.

Scanning a printed script

Text recognition runs on your device. The first time you use it, the app downloads the recognition library and its English language data from public content delivery networks (cdnjs by Cloudflare, jsDelivr and the Tesseract.js project). The photo of the page never leaves your device.

Signing in to Google, Apple, Dropbox or Microsoft

When you connect Google Drive or Google Calendar (or iCloud, Dropbox, OneDrive or Outlook, when Vdex offers them), you sign in on that service's own page, so Vdex never sees your password. For Google, Google's sign-in library loads from Google. For iCloud, Apple's page sends you back to Vdex with a session that Vdex keeps on your device, encrypted while Vdex is locked; it lasts about two weeks if you tick “Keep me signed in”. In the desktop app the sign-in page opens in your web browser and the answer comes back to Vdex through the app's own address on your computer. Vdex receives a token that lets it reach the files and calendars you allowed, and your account's name and email address, which stay on your device.

Vdex's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In plain words: Vdex uses what it receives from Google only to give you the storage and calendar features you switched on, on your own device. It is never sent to us, never sold, and never used for advertising.

Sharing

When you email a call sheet, a quote or an invoice, Vdex opens your mail app with the text ready to send. Printing and the share sheet hand the document to the app you choose. Vdex sends nothing itself.

Invoice, quote and crew links, contact card and equipment links, and payment buttons

When you make a link for a client, a "Share with crew" link for your cast and crew, a link to your contact card (the Contact card QR code on the Me page is one) or a link to equipment you share, everything is packed inside the link itself. A crew link always carries the shoot's title, type and dates, the call, lunch, break and wrap times, the schedule, the location and venue details (access, parking, power, nearest hospital), dress code, weather notes, the emergency contact, and your name, business name, phone number and email address from your profile. You choose whether it also carries the client's name; the crew (names, roles, call times, phone numbers and email addresses); the client's contacts and who is on set (off unless you tick it); the scenes from the stripboard, by day (scene numbers, sets, times of day, a one-line description, the cast names and page counts; never a strip's notes); the shot list; the equipment list (without values); the deliverables; the venue rules; and the call sheet instructions. It never carries prices, rates, budgets, quotes, invoices, kit values or your booking notes.

A contact card link carries your name, title, business, phone number, email address, website and where you are based. (The Me page's Phone contacts code holds the same details as a plain contact card, with no link, and its Website code holds only your website's address.) An equipment link carries, for the whole list, a kit or the items you tick: each item's name, category, serial number, where it is kept, quantity, value, purchase and warranty dates, service interval, firmware and notes, with your name and business, your currency symbol and the kits you included; never an item's service log or the bookings it is on. Serial numbers and values always go with it.

Opening one of these links loads only Vdex's own page files: the part of the link that holds the contents (after the # sign) is not sent to us or to any server. Vdex stores nothing online. The link itself holds the information, so it is kept wherever the link is kept (the email or message it was sent in, a browser's history, a printed QR code), not encrypted, until it is deleted there. A link that has been sent cannot be withdrawn. Anyone who has a link can read it, so share an invoice or quote link only with the client, a crew link only with your cast and crew, and an equipment link only with people you would hand that list to.

Someone who uses Vdex can add what a crew link, a contact card link or an equipment link holds to their own Vdex. Add to my Vdex on the link's page hands the link to Vdex in the same browser, on that device: the contents still never reach us. If Vdex is locked or has not finished starting, the link waits in that browser tab's own storage until you have seen what it holds, or the tab is closed. If you have linked cloud storage, Vdex first reads the copy in your own storage, for a few seconds at most, to see whether another of your devices has already added the same thing; that is an ordinary sync with your own storage, and nothing from the link goes there unless you add it. Vdex shows what a link holds before anything is added, and cannot confirm who made a link.

The Pay buttons on an invoice open the payment service you chose, such as PayPal, Venmo, Cash App or a payment page of your own, with the amount filled in where that service allows it, and for some (Venmo, Monzo, UPI and a payment page of your own) the invoice number as the payment's reference; what happens from there is between the client and that service, under its privacy policy. Vdex is never told whether a payment was made; you record payments yourself.

Linking two devices

When you link two devices with 🔗 Link device in timecode notes, each device asks a Google server (stun.l.google.com) for its own internet address so the two can find each other. Google receives your device's IP address and nothing else. The timecode log each device has open, with its notes and any script, then travels directly between the two devices.

The website, updates and the app stores

Vdex's files are served from vdex.crowmade.app by Cloudflare. When you open the app, or it checks for a new version, your browser requests files from Cloudflare, which keeps ordinary web server logs such as your IP address, browser type and the time, under its own privacy statement. The desktop app downloads itself, and checks for new versions, from download.crowmade.app, which Cloudflare also runs, in the same way. On Windows and Linux, the desktop app's spelling checker may download the dictionary for your language from Google the first time it is needed; the words you type are checked on your computer and are not sent. If you installed Vdex from Google Play or the App Store, Google or Apple gives us overall install and crash statistics. Those are not linked to anything inside Vdex.

Emails you send us

If you write to support@crowmade.app, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and keep the message in our mailbox so that we can answer you and deal with what you asked. Ask us and we delete it, unless the law requires us to keep it.

Licences: buying, activating and signing in

Vdex has a 30-day free trial on the website, in the desktop app and in the Android and iPhone apps, and then needs a licence. The trial is counted on your device; nothing is sent for it. The website and the Android and iPhone apps ask our licence server, at most every six hours, whether licences are switched on; that request carries nothing about you or your trial. During the trial, the website and the desktop app also ask it for the price with tax where you are, so that Buy can show the whole price: the server reads only the country the request comes from, as any website can, and our licence server keeps nothing from it (Cloudflare, which runs it, keeps its ordinary request logs, as for every request to vdex.crowmade.app). If you buy one, you pay on Stripe's checkout page: the seller is the one named on your receipt, and Stripe handles your payment details under its own privacy policy. We never see your card. Stripe gives us your name, email address and the order, which stay in our Stripe account. We put your name and email address inside your licence code, so Vdex can show whom it is licensed to, and send the code to your email address through Resend. If we give you a free licence, we keep your name, email address and the code in our own records.

When a code is activated on a computer, the desktop app sends our licence server at vdex.crowmade.app (run on Cloudflare) the code, a random number made for that installation of Vdex, and whether the computer is a Mac, Windows or Linux. For each code, the server keeps the list of those numbers, with the type of computer and the dates each was added and last checked, so a code can be used on up to 3 devices. It also notes codes that have been cancelled, for example after a refund. The app checks again about once a week; more often while it cannot get through (about once an hour) or while a code is waiting to be activated, has been moved to another computer or has been cancelled (about once a day), and not at all once a code has ended. Nothing else is sent: no hardware details, network names, location or anything from your work. Ask us and we will delete a code's list of computers. Two things about a licence are remembered on the device only, and are not sent: a computer on which a code was entered while it could not reach the server remembers when that code's 14-day wait began, and keeps remembering after the code is removed; and a device on which a licence has stopped working remembers the day it learned of it, so that Export backup can stay open for 30 days.

The Android app takes a place on the licence in the same way: after you sign in, it sends the server your sign-in, a random number made for that installation, and "Android". The iPhone app does the same when you sign in with an email address, sending "iPhone" or "iPad". An iPhone or iPad licensed by an App Store purchase takes no place and sends nothing for one.

The iPhone app can also buy the licence through the App Store. Apple takes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy; we never see your name, card or Apple ID. The app sends our licence server the receipt Apple signs for the purchase, which holds the purchase's number, date and product, the country of the App Store account and the price paid, but not your name or Apple ID. The server checks Apple's signature, makes the licence, and keeps only the purchase number and date with it, so the same purchase can be restored on your other iPhone or iPad, or after a reinstall. Apple also tells the server when such a purchase is refunded or revoked, so the licence is cancelled. A sign-in made this way holds the purchase number in place of an email address. If you add an email address to such a licence (Add your email in the app), a 6-digit code is emailed to it through Resend, and the address is then linked to the licence as a bought licence's is, so you can sign in with it elsewhere; ask us and we will remove it.

To sign in on the website or in the Android or iPhone app, you type your email address. If a licence belongs to it, our licence server emails you a 6-digit code through Resend; if none does, no email is sent. An address not yet linked to a licence is looked up among the orders in our Stripe account, in case it was used to buy one. The server keeps which email addresses belong to which licences (from purchases, from codes we give, and from codes activated in the desktop app), a scrambled form of each sign-in code with its tries, and when codes were sent, for up to a day, so no address gets more than 5 an hour or 4 a day; and a count of the sign-in emails sent each day. For the one address the app stores' reviewers sign in with, which takes a fixed code, the server also counts wrong codes by the internet address they came from, kept only in a scrambled form and for two days, so that the code cannot be found by trying. Once you are signed in, your browser keeps a signed sign-in note with your email address and your licence's number, and sends it to the server about once a day to check the licence is still good. Signing out deletes it from that browser. The link between your email address and your licence is kept, including after the licence ends or is cancelled, until you ask us to delete it, because signing in depends on it. It is made again whenever that email address or licence code is used, since the code holds the email address and the order holds it in Stripe; ask us and we will delete it with the licence's other records.

5. Device permissions and why Vdex asks for them

  • Camera: the white balance reader, the range finder, QR code scanning and script scanning. Images are processed on the device as you look at them, and are not stored or sent.
  • Microphone: the sound level meter and timecode sync listen live. Nothing is recorded or stored.
  • Location: sunrise and sunset times, the weather, and the "use my location" button for a venue. A position is kept only where you save it, such as in a venue or as your saved location, and it is sent only to Open-Meteo for the weather, rounded, as section 4 describes.
  • Notifications: break notifications from the shoot-day clock, shown by your device. There is no push server.
  • Calendars (the iPhone app): the "Vdex bookings" calendar Vdex makes in the Calendar app and keeps in step with your bookings. Vdex only uses that calendar; nothing from your other calendars is kept or sent anywhere.
  • Motion and orientation: aiming the phone at the sun or Polaris, and the range finder.
  • Files: importing and exporting backups, importing an equipment list from a spreadsheet (a CSV or Excel file), saving PDFs, and scripts or lists you share into Vdex. A file you import is read on your device and is not sent anywhere.
  • Fingerprint or face: unlocking, as described above.

Each permission is asked for the first time a feature needs it, and can be withdrawn at any time in your device's settings.

6. Children

Vdex is for adults: people who work in film and video, and people working towards it. It is not meant for anyone under 18, and we do not knowingly hold any information about children.

7. Your choices

  • Back up everything (with a licence), or erase everything, under Settings → Storage, sync & backups.
  • Unlink a cloud storage or calendar in Vdex, and withdraw Vdex's access in that provider's account settings.
  • Deny or withdraw any device permission in your device's settings.
  • Delete what our licence server keeps about you (the email address or App Store purchase your licence is linked to, and the devices on it): Settings → General → About & Licence → Delete my licence records, while you are signed in, or email us (how to ask, and what is kept). A computer that still has your licence code puts itself back on the licence at its next weekly check, so use Release this computer there first. We also have your order in our Stripe account, a note of any free licence we gave you, and emails you sent us: ask and we delete what the law lets us. Stripe, the seller named on your receipt and Apple keep their own records of a purchase under their own policies.
  • Do Not Track: Vdex does no tracking across websites or apps, and no third party collects information about your activity over time through it, so a browser's Do Not Track setting changes nothing.
  • Uninstalling Vdex does not always remove what it stored: the desktop app leaves its data on the computer, and the Google Play app's data is kept by the browser it runs in, usually Chrome; the iPhone app's data goes with the app when it is deleted. To remove it, use Settings → Storage, sync & backups → Erase all data on this device before you uninstall. Copies in your own cloud storage, including monthly backups, stay there until you delete them.

8. Security

Vdex is delivered over HTTPS, runs only code from the addresses listed above, and stores none of your work on any server of ours (the licence server keeps only what is described above). Your information is encrypted on the device whenever Vdex is locked, hidden or closed, within the limits section 3 lists: a short PIN can be guessed by someone who copies the device's storage, a few things are kept outside the PIN's protection, the copy in your cloud storage is plain text protected by your account there, and a full browser storage can leave the information unencrypted until space is freed.
